\chapter{Literature review}
\label{ch:literature-review}

This chapter establishes the theoretical and empirical foundation for integrating a retrieval-augmented AI assistant into Sovelia Core, an on-premise PLM system. The review first summarizes core characteristics of Product Lifecycle Management systems and the role of AI assistants in data-intensive work, motivating a human-in-the-loop approach for enterprise PLM environments (\autoref{sec:plm-systems-and-ai-assistants}). After this, the architecture and principles of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems are described, focusing on how combining parametric and non-parametric memory can reduce limitations of purely parametric models in knowledge-intensive tasks (\autoref{sec:resource-augmented-ai-systems}).

The core of this review examines five recent case studies of RAG-based systems deployed in technical documentation and manufacturing contexts (\autoref{sec:case-studies-on-similar-integrations}): Heredia Álvaro and Barreda's ceramic tile manufacturing quality control system, Shejuti et al.'s technical documentation chatbot, Knollmeyer et al.'s manufacturing documentation system with knowledge graph enhancement, Nam et al.'s LoRA-tuned multimodal RAG system for technical manual question answering, and Wang et al.'s comprehensive review of AI in PLM. Together, these studies suggest recurring success factors (notably domain adaptation and iterative evaluation) and persistent challenges (retrieval precision, query complexity, and governance). Because PLM deployments are common in manufacturing organizations \parencite{starkProductLifecycleManagement2015}, these results provide a relevant baseline for deriving design considerations and risks for the Sovelia Core assistant.

\section{Overview of PLM systems and AI assistants}
\label{sec:plm-systems-and-ai-assistants}

Product Lifecycle Management (PLM) systems are integral to managing the entire lifecycle of a product from inception, through engineering design and manufacturing, to service and disposal. They provide a centralized repository for all product-related information, facilitating collaboration among different departments and stakeholders \parencite{starkProductLifecycleManagement2015}. In PLM systems generally the lifecycle states of product include: imagine, define, realize, support/use and retire/dispose \parencite{starkProductLifecycleManagement2015a}. All these separate stages generate data (including documentation) that needs to be managed effectively. The purpose of PLM system is to centralize all this data and provide users with access to the right information at the right time.

AI assistants, in the context of data-intensive workflows, can be defined as \textit{semi-automatic interactive tools} that guide analysts by recommending transformations or actions based on interaction with the analyst \parencite{petricekAIAssistantsFramework2023}. This human-in-the-loop approach combines automation with critical human oversight, which is particularly suitable for enterprise workflows where domain knowledge and exceptions matter.

In this thesis, the aim is to develop an AI assistant that can be integrated into a PLM system to improve access to documentation and provide context-aware support, while maintaining human oversight and control.

Following Plaat et al.'s definition of agentic LLMs as systems that can reason, act (e.g., via tools), and interact \parencite{plaatAgenticLargeLanguage2026}, the assistant can be considered agentic in a limited sense because it may decide when to invoke retrieval tools and how to formulate retrieval queries. However, it is not fully autonomous: it operates on user-initiated requests and users remain responsible for decisions and any real-world actions taken based on the assistant's recommendations.

\section{Resource-augmented AI systems}
\label{sec:resource-augmented-ai-systems}

Resource-augmented AI systems, also known as \emph{retrieval-augmented generation} (RAG) systems, represent a paradigm shift in how artificial intelligence systems access and utilize knowledge. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding and generation \parencite{zhaoSurveyLargeLanguage2025}, yet they face fundamental challenges including hallucination, outdated knowledge, and inability to provide verifiable sources \parencite{gaoRetrievalAugmentedGenerationLarge2024}. Unlike purely parametric models that store all knowledge implicitly within their parameters, resource-augmented systems combine parametric memory (the weights of a neural network) with non-parametric memory (external knowledge sources such as databases, document collections, or knowledge graphs) \parencite{LewisPatrick2020Rgfk}. This hybrid architecture addresses several fundamental limitations of parametric-only models: the inability to easily update or revise stored knowledge, difficulty in providing provenance for predictions, and the tendency to generate hallucinated or factually incorrect content \parencite{LewisPatrick2020Rgfk}. In simplified terms, RAG systems can be viewed as AI systems that "look up" relevant information from an external source before generating a response, rather than relying solely on learned patterns from training data and thus enabling more accurate, up-to-date, and contextually relevant outputs. This will result in less hallucinations compared to standard large language models (LLMs) \parencite{LewisPatrick2020Rgfk}.

In a typical RAG architecture, the system consists of two main components: a \emph{retriever} that identifies relevant documents or passages from an external knowledge source given an input query, and a \emph{generator} that produces outputs conditioned on both the input and the retrieved content \parencite{LewisPatrick2020Rgfk}. The retriever typically employs dense passage retrieval methods using bi-encoder architectures that compute semantic similarity between queries and documents in a shared embedding space, while the generator is commonly implemented as a pre-trained sequence-to-sequence transformer model \parencite{VaswaniAshish2017Aiay}. Crucially, these components can be trained end-to-end, allowing the retrieval mechanism to learn what information is relevant for the downstream task without requiring explicit retrieval supervision. However, recent research has identified that language models struggle to effectively utilize information positioned in the middle of long contexts, with performance degrading significantly when relevant information does not appear at the beginning or end of the input \parencite{liuLostMiddleHow2024}. This finding has important implications for retrieval strategies and chunk ordering in RAG systems. \autoref{fig:rag-architecture} illustrates this architecture, showing how user queries flow through the retriever to access the knowledge base, and how retrieved context is combined with the query in the generator to produce responses.

\textcite{LewisPatrick2020Rgfk} have highlighted that the advantages of resource-augmented systems are particularly pronounced in knowledge-intensive tasks. There are tasks that humans could not reasonably perform without access to external knowledge sources. By maintaining knowledge in an explicit, inspectable, and easily updatable non-parametric form, these systems enable dynamic knowledge updates by simply replacing or modifying the external knowledge source (a process sometimes called "index hot-swapping" \parencite{LewisPatrick2020Rgfk}) without requiring costly model retraining and allowing for customizability for different needs. Additionally, the retrieved documents allow users to understand what information informed the system's response. In the context of PLM systems, this architecture is particularly well-suited for handling the extensive, evolving documentation ecosystem that characterizes enterprise software deployments. The customizable nature of RAG systems allow for tailoring the knowledge and thus the responses to the specific configurations and processes of different organizations using the PLM system.

\section{Case studies on similar integrations}
\label{sec:case-studies-on-similar-integrations}

Several recent implementations of RAG-based systems for technical documentation and manufacturing environments provide valuable insights for the design and development of an AI assistant for Sovelia Core, the main topic of this thesis. This section examines five representative case studies that demonstrate both the feasibility and challenges of integrating RAG technology into similarly complex technical domains.

\subsection*{Manufacturing quality control: Heredia Álvaro and Barreda's ceramic tile RAG system}

Heredia Álvaro and Barreda (2025) developed a RAG system for manufacturing quality control in the ceramic tile industry \parencite{herediaalvaroAdvancedRetrievalaugmentedGeneration2025}. The work is relevant because it targets a documentation-heavy domain with specialized processes and users who need fast, actionable guidance from procedural and defect-related knowledge.

The proposed approach relies on careful preprocessing of domain content and a retrieval pipeline that prioritizes external knowledge over the language model's parametric memory. A central design choice is a two-stage retrieval strategy, where an efficient initial retrieval step is followed by reranking to improve relevance. In evaluation, the authors report that domain-adapted retrieval improves answer quality compared to using a general-purpose model without access to the curated documentation. For Sovelia Core, the main implication is that maintainable improvements can be achieved through document structuring, retrieval tuning, and reranking, without requiring model fine-tuning.

\subsection*{Technical documentation: Shejuti et al.'s MODTRAN chatbot}

Shejuti et al. (2025) addressed the problem of navigating extensive technical documentation through their RAG-based chatbot for MODTRAN (Moderate resolution atmospheric TRANsmission) software, a domain characterized by complex scientific documentation similar to specialized PLM system manuals \parencite{shejutiExtendedAbstractEnhancing2025}. The document selection included a MODTRAN6 user manual, algorithm theoretical basis document (ATBD), and MODTRAN FAQ resources. This documentation complexity is comparable to enterprise PLM deployments with multiple document types serving different user needs.

The implementation follows a typical pipeline of document extraction, chunking, embedding, and vector retrieval over heterogeneous sources (manuals and FAQs). Evaluation is based on expert-created question--answer pairs and a comparison between generated responses and expert expectations \parencite{shejutiExtendedAbstractEnhancing2025}. A similar expert evaluation approach is used in this thesis, focusing on correctness and source grounding rather than similarity to reference answers, discussed further in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation-and-results}.

The study supports the practical value of a simple RAG baseline in technical documentation, while also highlighting a recurring limitation: retrieval noise can degrade answers, which motivates iterative refinement of chunking and retrieval configuration.

\subsection*{Manufacturing documentation: Knollmeyer et al.'s Document GraphRAG}

Knollmeyer et al. (2025) introduced Document GraphRAG, a novel framework that enhances RAG systems by incorporating knowledge graphs built upon a document's intrinsic structure into the retrieval pipeline, specifically targeting manufacturing domain documentation \parencite{knollmeyerDocumentGraphRAGKnowledge2025}. This study is particularly relevant to PLM integration as it addresses persistent challenges in retrieval precision and context selection that hinder RAG effectiveness in technical documentation environments. Notably, the research employs Design Science Research methodology, which is the same methodological approach used in this thesis, to design, implement, and evaluate the GraphRAG framework.

The framework differs from a naive chunk-based baseline by preserving document structure and using a graph representation to guide context selection. This structural awareness is intended to improve retrieval for queries that require information from multiple sections, without requiring model fine-tuning \parencite{knollmeyerDocumentGraphRAGKnowledge2025}.

Across the reported evaluations, the approach improves retrieval robustness over a flat retrieval baseline, especially for multi-hop or multi-section questions \parencite{knollmeyerDocumentGraphRAGKnowledge2025}. For Sovelia Core, the main implication is that document structure can be treated as a first-class signal for retrieval, which aligns with enterprise documentation patterns and offers a plausible improvement path after a stable text-based baseline has been established.

\subsection*{Technical manual QA: Nam et al.'s LoRA-tuned multimodal RAG system}

Nam et al. (2025) introduced a domain-adaptive multimodal RAG system for question answering over large, structured technical manuals, using Hyundai Staria maintenance documentation as a case study \parencite{namLoRATunedMultimodalRAG2025}. The focus differs from manufacturing knowledge graph augmentation: the contribution is primarily in aligning manual content (text and images) to retrieval and generation, and adapting the models to a specific technical domain using parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) \parencite{HuEdward2022LLAO}.

The evaluation design is particularly relevant to the evaluation methods used in this thesis because it combines automated similarity-based metrics with expert- and user-centered assessment \parencite{namLoRATunedMultimodalRAG2025}. Quantitatively, the study reports response quality improvements using BERTScore, cosine similarity, and ROUGE-L, comparing a baseline language model against a RAG-enabled variant. In addition to text similarity metrics, procedural correctness was reported (89,7\%), reflecting the importance of step ordering in maintenance instructions.

Qualitatively, the system was evaluated through a structured user study with 20 domain experts (military maintenance personnel) using a 5-point Likert scale across multiple dimensions, including response speed, information clarity, usability, and perceived reliability \parencite{namLoRATunedMultimodalRAG2025}. The reported overall satisfaction score was 4,4/5 (SD = 0,6). Semi-structured interviews were used to capture qualitative feedback beyond scalar ratings. In addition to end-user evaluation, interrater agreement was reported for intermediate supervision signals (e.g., sentence similarity judgements) and for manual image--text alignment quality, providing transparency into the reliability of curated training and retrieval data.

For an enterprise PLM documentation assistant, this combination of quantitative and qualitative evaluation is a useful reference point. However, similarity-to-reference metrics (e.g., ROUGE-L) depend on having suitable ground-truth answers and can be a weak proxy for correctness in real documentation support, where answers must be verifiable against sources rather than merely similar to a reference. This limitation motivates a mixed evaluation approach where expert assessment and source verification complement automated metrics, as applied in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation-and-results}.

\subsection*{AI in PLM: Wang et al.'s comprehensive review}

Wang et al. (2021) provided a comprehensive review of AI applications throughout the product lifecycle, examining how intelligent systems can support PLM in the design, manufacturing, and service stages \parencite{wangArtificialIntelligenceProduct2021}. While their review covers AI technologies broadly rather than RAG systems specifically, it establishes the wider context in which RAG-based documentation assistants operate within PLM environments.

The review surveys AI applications across the product lifecycle and highlights recurring constraints in industrial settings \parencite{wangArtificialIntelligenceProduct2021}. Of particular relevance to documentation assistants is the emphasis on knowledge access and user-facing decision support, where semantic retrieval is used to connect user queries to curated knowledge sources.

Several challenges identified by Wang et al. directly affect RAG deployment in PLM environments. They emphasize that data quality, algorithm interpretability, and security remain critical concerns for AI in manufacturing contexts. The need to integrate data across different PLM stages and systems presents a significant challenge, as does the requirement to make AI systems understandable and trustworthy for industrial users who may be skeptical of AI recommendations in engineering workflows. \parencite{wangArtificialIntelligenceProduct2021}

For Sovelia Core, the primary implication is that technical effectiveness alone is insufficient. Data quality, interpretability, and security constraints strongly shape which AI features are acceptable in production PLM environments \parencite{wangArtificialIntelligenceProduct2021}. These concerns align with an on-premise, source-grounded assistant design that prioritizes transparency and governance.

\section{Synthesis and implications for Sovelia Core PLM}

Collectively, the case studies in \autoref{sec:case-studies-on-similar-integrations} demonstrate that RAG-based assistants are feasible in documentation-heavy technical domains, but that practical performance depends more on the retrieval and data pipeline than on the language model alone. Across domains, modern embedding-based retrieval can provide useful baselines without requiring model fine-tuning. At the same time, small configuration choices in chunking and retrieval depth can substantially influence answer quality, which motivates iterative tuning supported by expert evaluation.

The literature also suggests a clear progression path. After a stable text-based baseline has been established, retrieval can be improved by exploiting structure that already exists in technical documentation. Graph-based approaches can incorporate hierarchical relationships between sections to reduce retrieval noise for multi-step queries \parencite{knollmeyerDocumentGraphRAGKnowledge2025}. Another extension path is multimodal retrieval and parameter-efficient domain adaptation when the underlying documentation includes essential visual information and when domain terminology differs from general-purpose corpora \parencite{namLoRATunedMultimodalRAG2025}.

For the initial deployment in Sovelia Core, these findings support prioritizing a straightforward vector-based retrieval architecture, careful tuning of chunking and retrieval parameters, and evaluation that combines quantitative indicators with expert feedback. In enterprise PLM environments, interpretability and security constraints remain central, and the assistant should therefore be designed to ground responses in retrieved sources and to operate within the on-premise governance model \parencite{wangArtificialIntelligenceProduct2021}.

Regulatory expectations further reinforce these requirements. The EU AI Act emphasizes risk-based compliance and transparency in deployed AI systems \parencite{sillbergEUAIAct2025}. For a RAG assistant in a PLM context, this primarily motivates source-grounded responses, traceable behavior, and access control aligned with organizational policies.
