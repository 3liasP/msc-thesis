\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

In recent years, the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) assistants into various software systems has gained significant attention. AI assistants have the potential to enhance user experience, streamline workflows, and improve decision-making processes. Product Lifecycle Management (PLM) systems, which manage the entire lifecycle of a product from inception to disposal \parencite{starkProductLifecycleManagement2015}, can particularly benefit from the integration of AI assistants. By leveraging AI technologies, PLM systems can provide users with intelligent support, automate routine tasks, and allow for better collaboration among various stakeholders. For instance, AI applications in PLM span product design, manufacturing, and service stages, offering opportunities for innovation while presenting challenges such as data governance and algorithm reliability \parencite{wangArtificialIntelligenceProduct2021}.

In the case of PLM systems, one of the key challenges is the fact that they often handle vast amounts of documentation, including standard operating procedures (SOPs), manuals, and technical specifications. Managing this documentation effectively is important for ensuring that users can access the information they need when they need it \parencite{starkProductLifecycleManagement2015}. However, the explosive growth of unstructured and heterogeneous data in PLM systems can lead to fragmentation and integration difficulties \parencite{wangArtificialIntelligenceProduct2021}. This makes it challenging to maintain up-to-date and accurate documentation, which is essential for effective decision-making and operational efficiency. Because of the adaptability of PLM systems to various industries and organizational needs, high-quality documentation has a key role in user training, compliance, and knowledge transfer since there is no one-size-fits-all solution for PLM implementations \parencite{starkProductLifecycleManagement2015a}. While PLM systems can be deployed in various environments, they often operate in on-premises environments with strict data governance policies \parencite{ugniusOnPremiseCloudHosting2023}, which can further complicate the integration of AI assistants.

To manage and utilize the PLM documentation effectively, there is a growing interest in developing AI assistants that can interact with PLM systems. These AI assistants can help users navigate the vast amounts of documentation, provide context-aware help for specific tasks, and allow for knowledge sharing among team members. To implement an effective context-aware AI assistant within a PLM system, addressing of several challenges is needed, including data integration, natural language processing, and user interface design. One promising approach to building such AI assistants is the use of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, which combine large language models (LLMs) with external knowledge bases to provide accurate and contextually relevant responses \parencite{LewisPatrick2020Rgfk}.

This research investigates the integration of a RAG-based AI assistant within Sovelia Core \parencite{DigitalSolutionsEngineering}, an on-premise PLM platform widely adopted across Nordic manufacturing enterprises. Sovelia Core presents a typical PLM knowledge management challenge: users must navigate fragmented documentation spanning vendor help documentation, release notes, and customer-specific standard operating procedures. The on-premise deployment model, driven by intellectual property protection and data sovereignty requirements common in manufacturing organizations, imposes strict constraints on AI implementation strategies. This thesis employs Design Science Research methodology \parencite{johannessonIntroductionDesignScience2021} combined with a single case study approach \parencite{yinCaseStudyResearch2018} to develop, implement, and evaluate a RAG system that operates entirely within customer-controlled infrastructure. The implemented architecture leverages PostgreSQL \parencite{PostgreSQL181Documentation2025} with pgvector \parencite{PgvectorPgvector2025} extension for vector storage, supports multiple LLM providers (including fully on-premises options via Ollama \parencite{Ollama}), and integrates a documentation pipeline that processes internal PLM documents, external vendor documentation, and web-based knowledge articles. The system design emphasizes maintainability, operational simplicity, and alignment with existing PLM access control mechanisms to ensure practical deployability in enterprise environments.

The primary objective of this research is to develop an AI assistant that is seamlessly integrated with a PLM system, specifically Sovelia Core, to enhance user experience and improve documentation management. The AI assistant will leverage RAG techniques to provide context-aware assistance based on the extensive documentation from various sources stored within the database of the PLM system. To achieve this objective, the research will focus on the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1 (Design)}: What RAG system architecture best fits Sovelia Core's on-prem, PostgreSQL-based environment and data governance constraints?
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (Process)}: How should customer documentation pipelines (parse $ \rightarrow $ chunk $ \rightarrow $ embed $ \rightarrow $ retire) be engineered for reliability, maintainability and cost efficiency?
  \item \textbf{RQ3 (Quality \& Risk)}: Which risks, particularly those related to access control and stale context, are most critical, and what mitigations work in practice?
\end{itemize}

By addressing these research questions, this study aims to contribute to the development of effective AI assistants for PLM systems, ultimately enhancing user experience and improving documentation management in complex industrial environments.

Chapter~\ref{ch:literature-review} establishes the theoretical foundation by examining PLM systems, RAG architectures, and five case studies of similar implementations in manufacturing and technical documentation contexts, revealing that simple retrieval approaches often suffice when documentation quality is adequate. Chapter~\ref{ch:research-methodology} details the Design Science Research approach combined with a single case study of Sovelia Core, describing the on-premise deployment constraints, user requirements, and evaluation strategies employed. Chapter~\ref{ch:system-design-and-implementation} presents the implemented five-layer architecture utilizing PostgreSQL with pgvector, provider abstraction supporting multiple LLM options, database-level access control integration, and an automated documentation pipeline processing multiple source types. Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation-and-results} reports expert evaluation results (mean scores 4,31--4,49 on a 5-point scale) and performance measurements comparing cloud-based and on-premise LLM deployment configurations, while documentation coverage emerges as the primary constraint on response quality. Finally, Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions} synthesizes theoretical and practical contributions, addresses each research question directly, acknowledges study limitations, and identifies promising directions for future research in RAG-based PLM documentation assistance.
